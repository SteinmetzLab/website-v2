Department of Neurobiology & Biophysics

Brain-wide circuits for perception, cognition and action

We record from thousands of neurons across the whole mouse brain at once — with Neuropixels electrophysiology, cortex-wide calcium imaging, and optogenetics — to work out how distributed circuits turn sensation into decisions.

Behind this text: 141 neurons recorded simultaneously, 90 s of a real Neuropixels recording from this lab. Each mark is one spike; rows are sorted by depth along the probe.

What we do

Four questions we are asking

Each one needs a different way of looking at a brain. Every panel below is real data from the lab and the collaborations it is part of.

  1. Behavior

    How do we decide actions based on what we see?

    Mice report what they saw by turning a wheel, which gives us thousands of precisely measured choices per session.

  2. Neuropixels

    What neurons across the brain contribute to vision and decision-making?

    Neuropixels probes record hundreds of neurons at once, many probes at a time, from cortex to midbrain.

  3. Widefield

    What are the brain dynamics that give rise to behavior?

    Activity crosses the cortex in coordinated patterns, including waves that sweep and rotate, which we follow with imaging and electrophysiology.

  4. Circuits

    What circuits underlie these computations?

    Optogenetics, anatomy and single-neuron reconstruction tell us which projections actually carry a signal, and what happens without them.
